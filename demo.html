How to read this

A town, three problems, and a lord who threw a party.

Elsewhere is an idle game played inside Claude Code. Real time moves the world — you close the terminal, the world keeps going, and when you come back Claude tells you what happened while you were gone. You answer in plain English. Claude translates it into engine calls and the dice decide.

What follows is one complete game, eight in-world days, start to settled. Every line attributed to the player is verbatim. Every roll and outcome comes from the engine's own ledger — nothing here was narrated into existence after the fact.

Left pane

The conversation. What the player typed, and what Claude reported back from the ledger.

Right pane

The board — a live artifact that republishes every turn. Fronts, clocks, purse, orders.

Below

The picker. Each turn was taken through a modal; the player's pick is shown against it.

Dashed panels

Where Claude inferred a cost for an improvised order, and why it priced it that way.

The one rule that makes it work: only the front you are watching reports real numbers. Everything else is a rumour or silence. The game is decided by where you point your attention — which is the whole joke of what follows.

DAY 1 · TURN 1

Turn 1 · a world with nothing in the ledger

A front is a faction with a ten-segment clock. When the clock fills, they get what they want — and the world ends on whoever gets there first.

Claude

Fen's Reach still stands. Four hands, two hundred coin, a roof, and three problems that haven't decided what they are yet.

The Iron Hand — raiders, burning waystations one a week, working toward a coast road that belongs to nobody but them. You have eyes on them: clock 0, ten days.

The Ninth Watch — diggers, sinking a shaft nobody was asked about. No eyes. No numbers.

The Quiet Choir — zealots, singing at the barrow mouth in shifts. No eyes. No numbers.

This is the cleanest the board will ever look.

Raiders do not put a blade through a naked man walking uphill at them at dawn. They laughed. Then they traded. You came home 19 coin richer than you left, which is not how duels normally conclude.

✅ Walls and shutters — okroll 5 · 50 coin · fortify 3
✅ Coin into the reach — okroll 5 · 40 coin · invest running

Watched is −1 to a front's roll. Disrupted is −2 more. Stack them and a clock's rate goes to zero — not slower, stopped.

The Iron Hand, three ticks running: roll 3 +2 expansive −1 watched = 4 ✗ roll 6 +2 expansive −1 watched −2 disrupt = 5 ✗ roll 1 +2 expansive −1 watched −2 disrupt = 0 ✗

That middle line is the beat. They rolled a 6 and still failed.

The drift penalty didn't matter. The thin scale didn't matter. The Quiet Choir is back to clock 0 — a wipe of 5 against a clock of 2 doesn't reverse their progress, it erases the whole undertaking.

The disrupt that came with it is 4, the biggest number on the board, running twenty-four ticks. Their aggression drops to 1. They would roll thirteen more times and never move a segment again. They would roll sixes. It would not matter.

The reckoning · the one moment the fog lifts

1019 REMEMBERED badge: sun

Fen's Reach stands. Standing intact, 209 coin, ten hands, five investments running, walls never once tested. Not a single hand lost in eight days — and two of those days involved naked men walking at raiders on purpose.

The player beat the apocalypse under the barrow with one throw of twelve. Held the diggers to 6 of 10 for a week. Made all three factions beholden, and not one of them ever laid a hand on the walls.

They simply were not looking at the road.

The Iron Hand advanced nine of their ten segments while unwatched. Three times the road stood still: exactly the three ticks somebody was watching it. Every other tick, it moved.

It is not a defeat, exactly. Nobody died. It is just that somewhere out past the waystations, a faction of raiders finished a job while the entire population of Fen's Reach was extremely busy, for seventy-two consecutive hours, with erections and quilts.

All erections are good erections, bront.
